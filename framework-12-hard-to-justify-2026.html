Jeff Geerling has published his thoughts on the Framework 12, and the verdict is surprisingly lukewarm. Despite Framework's reputation as the poster child for repairable, modular laptops, the new 12-inch model apparently struggles to justify its price tag.

Why homelabbers should care

Framework laptops have always appealed to the tinkerer crowd. Swappable ports, upgradeable RAM and storage, and a company that actively encourages you to repair rather than replace — it's the right-to-repair dream. Many of us run these as portable SSH terminals, lightweight Proxmox management machines, or Ansible control nodes.

The problem, according to Geerling, is value. The Framework 12 lands in an awkward spot where you're paying a meaningful premium for modularity over comparably specced machines that simply cost less. If you're not actively swapping expansion cards or planning a motherboard upgrade down the line, the maths just don't work.

Honest caveats

Framework's mission is genuinely admirable, and if longevity and repairability are your top priorities, the premium might be worth it to you. But for most homelabbers on a budget — and let's be honest, that's most of us — a refurbished ThinkPad or a mini PC like a second-hand Lenovo ThinkCentre M720q will get you further per pound spent. Those tiny form-factor PCs double as headless nodes, and you can pick up two or three for the price of one Framework.

It's also worth noting that the 12-inch screen size limits its usefulness as a primary machine. It's firmly in the ultraportable category, which narrows the audience further.
