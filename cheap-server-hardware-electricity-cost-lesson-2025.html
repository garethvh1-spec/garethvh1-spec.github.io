We've all been tempted by a £50 Dell PowerEdge or a sub-£100 HP ProLiant on eBay. But as this XDA piece highlights, the real cost of older server hardware shows up on your electricity bill — and with UK energy prices still elevated in 2025, the maths matters more than ever.

The core lesson: older Xeon-based servers can idle at 150–300W. Run that 24/7 at current UK rates (~24p/kWh) and you're looking at £315–£630 a year just in power. A modern mini PC like an Intel N100 box idles at 6–10W — roughly £15–£20 a year. The "cheap" server pays for an efficient replacement in wasted electricity within months.

This is especially relevant for common homelab tasks like running Proxmox, a NAS, Pi-hole, or a handful of Docker containers. None of that needs dual-socket Xeons from 2015. A modern low-power box handles it comfortably with a fraction of the heat and noise too — your household will thank you.

Caveats: If you genuinely need ECC RAM, lots of PCIe lanes, or heavy multi-threaded grunt (Plex transcoding for a dozen streams, big ZFS pools), older rack gear can still make sense. Just go in with eyes open. Check the idle wattage with a plug-in power meter before committing, and factor annual running costs into your "bargain" purchase price.

What to do: Before buying any used server, plug the idle wattage into a quick yearly cost calculation. For lightweight services, look at mini PCs like the Intel N100 mini PC — they've become the sensible default for UK homelabs. And if you're repurposing an old laptop (built-in UPS, low power), that's a solid shout too.
