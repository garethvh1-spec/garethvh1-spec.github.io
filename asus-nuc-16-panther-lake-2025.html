ASUS has officially shown off its NUC 16 family, the next generation of compact PCs now powered by Intel's upcoming Panther Lake processors. For anyone running a homelab on mini PCs, this is one to bookmark.

The NUC line has been the go-to for low-power, small-footprint homelab nodes ever since Intel handed the brand to ASUS. Panther Lake brings a new CPU architecture promising better efficiency and integrated AI acceleration — though real-world benchmarks are still to come. The NUC 16 range appears to span performance and enthusiast tiers, so there should be options whether you're after a quiet Proxmox host or a capable Plex box.

Why homelabbers should care

Mini PCs are the sweet spot for many home setups: low idle power, near-silent, and small enough to stack on a shelf. Each NUC generation tends to bump up core counts and I/O without growing the chassis. If Panther Lake delivers on efficiency claims, running a cluster of these could be genuinely cheaper on the electricity bill than older gear — something that matters more every year with UK energy prices.

Caveats

These are showcase units right now — no confirmed UK pricing or availability yet. History says NUC launches carry a premium over equivalent Lenovo or Dell mini PCs, and first-batch stock can be patchy. If you're buying today, current-gen NUC 14 or Lenovo ThinkCentre Tiny units on clearance are still hard to beat on value. Don't wait around if your project needs hardware now.

It's also worth noting that "AI acceleration" features in the CPU are mostly irrelevant for typical homelab workloads like containers, NAS duties, or network services. Nice to have, not a reason to upgrade.
