Framework's modular laptops have always appealed to the tinker-minded crowd, and the new Framework 12 is no exception on paper. But as Jeff Geerling lays out in his latest write-up, actually pulling the trigger is another matter entirely.

The Framework 12 keeps the brand's hallmark repairability and swappable expansion cards, shrunk into a smaller form factor. For homelabbers, that modularity is genuinely attractive — swap in an Ethernet card, add extra USB-C ports, or slot in storage as your needs change. The right-to-repair angle means you're not binning the whole machine when one component dies.

The problem? Price. Geerling's core argument is that once you spec a Framework 12 to a usable config, you're paying a significant premium over comparable off-the-shelf laptops. For a dedicated homelab admin machine or a portable SSH terminal, that's a hard sell when a refurbed ThinkPad does the job for a fraction of the cost. The smaller chassis also means thermal compromises, and the 12-inch screen won't win any fans for extended dashboard monitoring.

There's also the ecosystem question. Framework's expansion card market is growing but still niche. You're betting on the company's longevity every time you invest in their platform — fair enough for a startup that's proven more durable than many expected, but still worth weighing up.

Who should care? If you absolutely want a repairable, upgradeable compact laptop and you're willing to pay the modularity tax, the Framework 12 delivers on its promise. If you just need something functional for managing your rack, your money goes further elsewhere.
