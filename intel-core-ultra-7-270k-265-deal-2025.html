Intel's Core Ultra 7 270K — part of the Arrow Lake desktop lineup — has dropped to $265 (roughly £210 at current exchange), making it one of the more interesting mid-range CPUs for a new homelab build right now.

The 270K is a 20-thread chip (8P + 12E cores) with no integrated graphics on the K-series, but it brings Intel's latest architecture with notably improved power efficiency over Raptor Lake. For homelabbers, that matters. If you're building a Proxmox host, a beefy Docker box, or a development workstation that runs 24/7, the lower idle and load power draw translates directly to reduced electricity bills — something UK homelabbers feel keenly.

At this price point it undercuts AMD's competing Ryzen 7 9700X in most regions while trading blows on multi-threaded performance. You'll need a new LGA 1851 motherboard and DDR5 RAM, so this isn't a drop-in upgrade for existing builds. Factor in a B860 board (starting around £140) and 32 GB DDR5 (around £80), and you're looking at a capable platform for under £450 on the core components.

Caveats: This is a US deal price spotted by XDA. UK pricing tends to lag, so check Intel Core Ultra 7 270K for current local stock. The K-series also requires a discrete GPU — fine if you've already got one, but an extra cost if not. And Arrow Lake's gaming reputation has been lukewarm, though that's largely irrelevant for server and virtualisation workloads where it performs well.

If you're planning a new homelab build in 2025, this is worth watching. DDR5 and LGA 1851 give you a platform with legs, and sub-£220 for a 20-thread CPU is genuinely good value. Keep an eye on UK retailers for similar discounts landing here — they usually follow within a few weeks.
