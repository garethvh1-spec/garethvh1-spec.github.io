The Minisforum N5 Max has landed with AMD's flagship Ryzen AI Max+ 395, and ServeTheHome's review is worth your time if you've been eyeing a compact local AI or virtualisation box.

The headline spec is 128GB of unified memory shared between the 16-core Zen 5 CPU and the integrated Radeon 8060S GPU (40 RDNA 3.5 CUs). That's the same memory pool, laptop-style — which makes this genuinely interesting for running large language models locally without shelling out for a discrete GPU with enough VRAM. Think 70B-parameter models that would normally demand a used enterprise GPU or multiple cards.

Beyond AI, the N5 Max is a credible homelab node: dual 2.5GbE NICs, up to two M.2 NVMe slots, and enough grunt for a Proxmox host running a stack of VMs and containers. The small footprint means it'll sit on a shelf rather than dominating a rack.

Caveats are real though. This is not cheap — expect north of £2,000 for the top-spec config. Cooling a 120W+ mobile chip in a mini chassis means fan noise under sustained load. And while 128GB unified memory sounds brilliant for LLMs, memory bandwidth is still well below what a dedicated GPU offers, so inference speed won't match an NVIDIA RTX 4090 in raw throughput. You're trading outright speed for the ability to fit bigger models in memory at all.

For a UK homelabber who wants a quiet-ish, compact box that doubles as a local AI workstation and a hypervisor, this is one of the most interesting mini PCs released this year. Just go in with realistic expectations on thermals and price.
